Controls

Click the surface to apply a localized pressure source. The resulting waves propagate, disperse, interfere, and dissipate according to the selected fluid. The menus, axes toolbar, parameter controls, and keyboard shortcuts provide access to the fluid and source properties, display options, and playback controls.

Menus

The Experiment, Source, View, Playback, and Help menus provide access to the app's controls. Frequently used commands also appear on the axes toolbar.

Command Effect
Playback > Pause / Resume Pause or resume the waves and scheduled rain.
Source > Rain > Create rain Schedule twenty clicks over 0.95 seconds of elapsed real time while playback runs.
Source > Rain > Pressure multiplier Scale the pressure strength for rain relative to a manual click; default 1x.
Source > Auto-click > Enable Create ripples as the pointer moves over the surface.
Source > Auto-click > Pressure multiplier Scale the pressure strength for Auto-click relative to a manual click; default 0.2x.
Experiment > Clear surface Remove waves and cancel pending rain without resetting simulation time or the rain generator.
Source > Rain > Random seed Set the private generator used by subsequent rain bursts.
View > Save Screenshot Save the displayed surface and visible controls to a PNG file.

On platforms with native menu access keys, press Alt to reveal their underlines. Alt+E, Alt+S, Alt+V, Alt+P, and Alt+H open Experiment, Source, View, Playback, and Help. The underlined keys in each submenu select its commands.

Experiment

Experiment > New scaled experiment selects the gravity, balanced, or capillary water setup. Each setup defines the fluid, physical domain, source, playback rate, and height scale. A checkmark identifies a setup whose preset values match the current settings.

The Material submenu selects water, an oil grade under Oil, acacia honey, molten chocolate, or a custom Newtonian fluid. Experiment > Temperature adjusts water temperature from 0 to 40 °C, with presets and Custom.... The submenu is enabled for water. Olive oil uses room-temperature measurements at a nominal temperature of 23 °C; silicone oils use manufacturer data at 25 °C. The oil presets do not provide a temperature correlation. The honey sample is specified at 30 °C. Molten chocolate uses a constant-viscosity approximation at a nominal 60 °C. Use Experiment > Material > Custom to enter measured properties and their temperature. These operations start a new experiment and clear existing waves.

Selecting a named material restores its fluid properties, wavelength, pressure strength, height scale, and appearance, including when the same material is selected again. The domain, grid, excitation method, and playback rate are preserved. Equal pressure loading does not imply equal wave height in different fluids. See Material presets for the property values and measurement conditions.

Selecting a material enables surface lighting. Presets also restore their default surface color; custom materials retain the selected color.

Experiment > Physical size selects the domain width in millimeters, with presets and Custom.... Changing the physical size starts a new experiment and clears existing waves.

Experiment > Grid resolution provides presets and a custom node count. Changing resolution resamples the existing wave field at its current physical time. Increasing the count gives finer spatial sampling and increases drawing and sampling costs; it does not change the physical size or speed of the waves. A requested grid must resolve every active source as well as the source selected for new clicks. Increase the grid resolution, choose a larger characteristic wavelength, or reduce the physical domain size to satisfy this sampling requirement.

Experiment > Wave reflection contains Reflecting boundaries and Reflection coefficient, which select the boundary model and reflected amplitude fraction. The Reflecting boundaries axes-toolbar button and B toggle the same boundary setting.

Experiment > Restore default experiment restores the initial physical settings, grid, appearance, camera, playback, sound, and random seed.

See Experiments for numerical comparisons of physical scale, fluid properties, and reflecting boundaries.

Source

Source > Excitation method selects a pressure impulse or a four-cycle wavemaker. The Source menu also sets the characteristic wavelength and pressure strength of subsequent inputs. A localized source excites a spectrum of wavelengths; the characteristic wavelength is not the wavelength of every visible ring.

Changing the source settings leaves existing waves unchanged. For example, change the wavelength between two clicks to compare the resulting wave propagation in the same fluid. During rain, these changes affect the clicks that have not yet occurred. The separate rain multiplier applies to newly scheduled bursts.

Source > Pressure strength controls the physical amplitude. For unchanged fluid properties, wavelength, and excitation method, the surface displacement is directly proportional to the pressure strength. The presets are 25, 50, 75, 100, 150, and 200 mPa·s. Choose Custom... to enter another value in millipascal-seconds. In Pressure impulse mode, this is the time integral of the pressure at the center of an individual Gaussian source. In Four-cycle wavemaker mode, it is a pressure-time scale; multiplying it by the reference angular frequency gives the pressure amplitude coefficient before the envelope is applied. See Source definitions for the forcing equations.

The Pressure strength submenu provides preset values and a custom value. The checkmark identifies the selected pressure strength.

A rain burst applies twenty localized sources. By default, each has the same pressure strength, wavelength parameter, and excitation method as a manual click. Its pressure multiplier offers 0.25x, 0.5x, 0.75x, 1x, 1.5x, 2x, and Custom.... Each burst retains its selected multiplier; other source settings follow their values at each input time. Repeated bursts overlap and retain all previously scheduled drops. Pausing partway through a burst preserves its remaining scheduled clicks. Experiment > Clear surface cancels the pending schedule along with the waves. Falling streaks mark the impact positions in oblique views. Top view hides the streaks while preserving the impacts and resulting ripples.

Source > Auto-click > Enable, the Auto-click axes-toolbar button, or Space enables pointer-driven ripples. Each new accepted position uses the selected Auto-click pressure multiplier, with at least 0.05 seconds of elapsed real time between inputs. Remaining over the same grid node does not repeat the input. Auto-click operates in Ripple pointer mode during playback; pause and camera navigation suspend it.

Source > Auto-click > Pressure multiplier offers 0.1x, 0.2x, 0.5x, 1x, 1.5x, 2x, and Custom.... The default is 0.2x: one fifth of the selected pressure strength. This multiplier affects new Auto-click inputs independently of the Rain multiplier. Existing waves retain their pressure loading.

View

View > Surface view provides Solid surface (A), Wireframe (W), Colormap (S), and High contrast (D).

The View menu groups surface appearance, lighting, and camera controls. The expanded submenu identifies the selected surface view.

Selecting Wireframe enables lighting and displays black mesh edges. With lighting enabled, white faces show the surface shape; with lighting disabled, the faces are transparent. Both presentations use the same mesh. Leaving Wireframe restores the previous surface view and its default lighting: enabled for Solid surface and High contrast, disabled for Colormap. In Solid surface, turning lighting off shows a transparent mesh in the selected surface color; white surfaces use black edges for visibility. Turning lighting on restores the filled surface without edge lines. These choices only affect the display.

Wireframe view of the same two-source wave field, with lighting enabled.

View > Transparency sets the filled surface to 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, or 100% opaque, with Custom... for any percentage in that range. At 10% the surface is highly transparent; 100% is fully opaque. Wireframe and views with lighting disabled ignore this setting. Returning to a lit surface restores the selected opacity. Selecting a named material sets 100% opacity. Changing the surface color retains the selected opacity. Transparency changes only the display.

Selecting High contrast also enables lighting; the lighting toggle remains available. High contrast maps elevations above a fixed positive threshold to white and the remaining elevations to black. Lighting provides additional surface-shape cues. Colormap and High contrast use a fixed range in the displayed vertical coordinates, so fading waves are not automatically recolored to appear stronger.

View > Colormap selects the colors for Colormap (S). Choose a MATLAB colormap available in the current release, reverse its order, or choose Custom... to enter a function name or RGB matrix. Each matrix row defines one color with red, green, and blue components between 0 and 1. For example, [0 0 1; 0 1 1; 1 1 1] defines blue, cyan, and white. A single RGB triplet defines a constant-color map. Selecting a colormap activates Colormap. The chosen map is retained when switching to another view and back. Color limits remain fixed as waves decay.

Full screen, available in the View menu, axes toolbar, and through F11, shows only the surface. Menus, the axes toolbar, information panels, and parameter controls are hidden. Press F11 again or Escape to restore them with their previous settings. Outside full screen, Escape closes the app. X closes the app in either window mode.

View > Height scale selects the vertical display multiplier. A value of 1 uses equal physical scales in the horizontal and vertical directions. Larger values make small surface slopes visible in the lighting. The calculation, physical quantities, and stored elevation remain unchanged. The multiplier does not vary automatically with the current wave amplitude. Menu presets are 20x, 50x, 75x, 100x, 150x, and 200x. Custom... accepts values from 1x to 1,000,000x.

Automatic, enabled when the app opens, adjusts the multiplier when fluid properties or characteristic wavelength change. Equal reference pressure impulses are compared with water at 20 °C, whose scale is 100x. This makes small viscous responses visible without changing their physical amplitude or damping. The scale stays fixed as waves propagate and settle; pressure strength changes remain visible. Selecting a numeric multiplier or a named material disables Automatic; selecting Automatic recalculates the multiplier. Use the same manual scale when comparing physical wave amplitudes between fluids.

View > Display status shows or hides the explanatory text and numerical measurements. The bottom panel identifies the domain, fluid, temperature, density, grid, playback rate, and height scale. The same screen footprint can represent different physical sizes; use the dimensions shown in the bottom panel when comparing experiments.

Crest elevation is the highest sampled elevation relative to the still water level. Trough depth is the greatest depth below that level, reported as a nonnegative value. Wave height is the difference between the highest and lowest sampled elevations. All three quantities are expressed in millimeters before vertical exaggeration. The accompanying text explains the selected control or suggests a physical comparison.

View > Surface color changes color and lighting independently of the physical fluid. Choose a basic MATLAB color or Dark brown, Brown, Ivory, Amber, Golden, Caramel, or Sand. Custom color... opens MATLAB's color picker. The same picker is available under Experiment > Material > Custom. Selecting a color switches to Solid view with lighting enabled.

The interface initially follows MATLAB's figure theme. Use View > Theme to select Light, Dark, or MATLAB Desktop while the app is running. MATLAB Desktop restores automatic selection from MATLAB's Figure Theme setting, which follows the desktop unless a figure-specific preference is set. The selected menu item shows whether the app follows this setting or uses an explicit light or dark theme. The submenu appears only when the installed release supports figure themes. Dark mode uses charcoal panels, light text, and contrasting toolbar icons. Theme changes preserve the surface colors, colormap, camera, and wave field. On MATLAB releases without figure theme support, the interface uses its light palette.

View > Background Color selects the color behind the surface. The default follows the figure theme, or uses white on releases without theme support. Choose a basic MATLAB color or use Custom color... to open the color picker. Theme follows the figure theme; this choice appears only on releases with theme support. Fixed colors remain unchanged when the interface theme changes. The background does not affect the surface color or the wave field.

Playback

View > Show FPS controls the frame-rate display in the bottom panel. It is on by default. The value updates during playback and uses the reciprocal of the mean of the last ten frame intervals. Pausing displays FPS: 0; resuming starts a fresh measurement. Frame rate measures display smoothness, not wave speed.

The playback rate ranges from 0.1x to 2x. Gravity and Balanced start at 0.25x; Capillary starts at 0.10x. It sets how quickly simulation time passes relative to real time. At 0.1x, one second in the simulation takes ten seconds to watch. Changing the frame rate does not change wave speed or the rain schedule.

The simulation pauses while the app calculates a new wave response. It resumes afterward if playback was running before the calculation. Time spent paused does not count toward the simulation. During playback, a delayed frame still shows the state at the correct elapsed time. The displayed time corresponds to the most recently calculated state.

Manual clicks and Auto-click share 16 overlapping splash voices. Each rain burst plays one recording of rain striking water. Repeated bursts can overlap without restarting an earlier recording. The speaker toolbar button, M, and Playback > Sound mute or unmute both recordings. See Sound for recording playback, overlap, and audio preparation.

Help

The Help menu opens the manual, controls, worked examples, physics of ripples, material properties, numerical methods, and programming reference.

Axes toolbar

The axes toolbar buttons provide camera restore, rotate/ripple mode, pause, rain, Auto-click, clear, reflections, wireframe, lighting, sound, parameter controls, and full screen. The lighting button uses a white bulb interior when lighting is on and a dark interior when it is off. Its selected state follows the lighting setting. The menus and toolbar reflect the same application settings. Surface clicks, Auto-click, and rain requests are ignored while paused.

The axes toolbar provides direct access to playback, ripple sources, surface appearance, and camera controls. The first button switches between Pause and Resume.

Parameter controls

View > Parameter controls shows or hides the wavelength, pressure strength, and playback sliders and numeric fields. These controls are visible at startup. Slider changes are applied when you release the slider thumb.

The pressure strength slider starts at 10 mPa·s. Its upper endpoint is 200 mPa·s, or the selected pressure strength if greater. The adjacent numeric fields accept custom values subject to the source and sampling limits.

Mouse interaction

Choose the pointer mode from View > Pointer mode. In Ripple mode, a click on the displayed surface creates a source. In Rotate, Pan, or Zoom mode, dragging changes only the camera. Camera motion does not change the observation domain, wavelength, grid, or wave state.

The mouse wheel zooms over the surface. Restore camera returns to the original oblique angle and fits the surface to the available viewport. This close view adjusts to window and panel resizing. Camera view also provides top and wide oblique views.
