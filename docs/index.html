Ripple Simulator

Ripple Simulator is an interactive MATLAB app for visualizing surface gravity waves and capillary wave ripples. Explore how gravity, surface tension and viscosity shape waves on a fluid's surface. Click the surface to create ripples, and watch a localized disturbance separate into waves of different wavelengths and speeds. Overlap multiple sources or trigger a burst of rain to produce wave interference patterns, and vary the fluid and wave properties to investigate wave propagation, dispersion, reflection, and viscous dissipation.

Water at 20 °C; 50 mm characteristic wavelength in a 650 × 650 mm domain; 801×801 nodes, 100x height scale, and 0.25x playback, exported at 50 frames per second.

Quick start

Add the project folder to the MATLAB search path, then run:

app = rippleSimulator();

Click the surface to create a ripple. Press P to pause or resume, R for a burst of rain, or C to clear. The menus and axes toolbar provide the same actions. The wavelength, pressure strength, and playback controls are visible initially; View > Parameter controls shows or hides them.

The bottom panel reports the domain size, fluid, temperature, grid resolution, playback rate, and selected height scale. Contextual explanations describe the physical or visual effects of each selection. Live measurements of crest elevation, trough depth, and wave height support comparisons between experiments.

Learning objectives

The guided experiments help you interpret the observed wave motion. After working through them, you should be able to:

Experiment Learning objective
Observe the wave field by creating a ripple. Examine how the surface elevation changes as the ripple propagates.
Compare physical scales by examining the relative contributions of the restoring forces of gravity and surface tension. Identify the gravity and capillary wave regimes.
Compare phase and group velocities by using the gravity–capillary wave dispersion relation. Explain why individual wave crests can move differently from the wave-packet envelope.
Investigate wave interference by creating overlapping ripples. Visualize constructive and destructive interference through linear superposition.
Investigate wave reflection by varying the reflection coefficient. Quantify how the wave amplitude and energy change after successive reflections.
Compare different fluids by applying identical pressure impulses. Assess the combined effects of the viscosity, density, and surface tension on the propagation and amplitude of the waves.
Isolate the effect of viscosity by varying it while keeping the other fluid properties and source settings constant. Observe how viscous dissipation reduces the wave amplitude and determine why shorter-wavelength components decay more rapidly.

Make a prediction before changing a setting. Compare the resulting field with that prediction, using the physical measurements and the explanations on the bottom panel. The scientific guide supplies the equations and their assumptions.

Explore

Freely explore additional concepts. Create ripples using a pressure impulse or a four-cycle wavemaker. Combine sources at different positions, vary their characteristic wavelength and pressure strength, or use rain to produce overlapping waves. Try to answer the following questions:

  1. Why do wave rings separate? Select the gravity water setup, create a ripple and follow its expanding pattern. Compare the motion of individual crests with the outward motion of the wave packet. Different spectral components travel at different phase and group velocities. Changing the characteristic wavelength changes the wave spectrum.
  2. What changes at smaller scales? Compare the gravity and capillary wave setups. Each setup represents a different wave regime. Gravity is the dominant restoring force for the long waves, while surface tension becomes more important at shorter wavelengths.
  3. What is the role of viscosity? Compare water at 25 °C with the specified silicone oils while keeping the source settings constant. Observe how viscous dissipation acts to reduce the wave amplitude and suppress oscillations in strongly damped wave components.
  4. What happens when it rains? Generate a rain burst of twenty ripples at random positions. Observe how the overlapping sources produce constructive and destructive interference. Repeat with different random seeds to compare the resulting interference patterns.
  5. What happens when waves reach a boundary? Enable wave reflection and create a ripple near a boundary. Use the top view to observe the reflected waves and their interference with the outgoing waves. Compare reflection coefficients of 100% and 50% to examine how successive boundary encounters affect a wave component’s amplitude and energy.
  6. How do the waves differ between fluids? Compare water, olive oil, two silicone oil grades, and Newtonian approximations for honey and molten compound chocolate by applying identical pressure impulses. Keep the domain and height scale constant, and examine the wave propagation and amplitude at the same elapsed time. Use a custom fluid to vary one property at a time.
  7. How does the excitation method affect the wave field? Apply a pressure impulse, observe the wave field, and then repeat with a four-cycle wavemaker at the same position. Keep the fluid properties, characteristic wavelength, and pressure strength constant. Observe the differences in the formation and propagation of the resulting wave packets.
  8. How does spatial resolution affect the sampled physical quantities? Pause playback, then refine the observation grid while preserving the existing wave state. Use the wireframe view to visualize the effect of spatial resolution on the sampled physical quantities.

Ripple Simulator models gravity–capillary waves on a homogeneous, incompressible Newtonian fluid of infinite depth based on small-amplitude wave theory. The full viscous response is obtained by solving the linearized Navier–Stokes and continuity equations subject to the linearized kinematic and dynamic free-surface boundary conditions. The source code is available in the project repository. See Installation and requirements for prerequisites, setup instructions, and troubleshooting.
